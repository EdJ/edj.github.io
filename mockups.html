The other half of the editor: a scale-locked piano roll, eight scale degrees tall, both lanes sharing the same C4–C5 rows. Pitch names sit on the first column — root rows a shade brighter — and on placed notes; the beat line splits the bar. Notes take the instrument's magenta with chord voicings ghosted above the root, and the selected note is the one rounded shape in the whole app. The translucent cyan column is the playhead, and each lane keeps its own step row at the bottom.

The slicer with a break loaded. Eight slices divide the waveform, the unsliced head is painted out at 0.6 ground the way SlicedWaveform does it, and slice five is bracketed in the voice’s own teal — the one that plays when a step carries no slice of its own. Tune is the two-cell coarse/fine control from SamplerTuneEditor, semitones then cents, and the panel foot carries the only two panels the sampler actually has.

The GEN mode that writes harmony. Chords are stored as scale DEGREES, not pitches, so the whole progression follows the project’s C minor — each block carries its name, its resolved notes, and a voicing line where it fits. Both four-bar rows are always drawn and the region past Bars is hatched out, so dragging Bars never moves anything under your finger. The square dots along the bottom are the note pattern’s firing steps: twelve steps against a sixteen-step bar, which is the polymetry made visible.
