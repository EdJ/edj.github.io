01

The step owns the instrument

One voice · 16 steps

On most grooveboxes a track is one sound for the whole pattern. Here the assignment lives on the step. One lane can hold a kick, a hat, a snare and a chord — and eight lanes run in parallel.

02

The same sixteen steps, in the app

Pattern editor · iPhone

The mechanic is not buried in a menu. It is the grid you spend your time in: tap an instrument, tap a step. Everything that shapes that instrument sits beside it.

03

Four instruments, one grammar

Assignable to any step of any voice

Every instrument is edited through the same panel, the same dials, the same chips. Learn one and you have learned all four.

Classic SynthIns 01

Wavetable. Spread, 2-op FM, paraphony, in-scale chords.

Macro SynthIns 02

Sixteen sub-synths, after a classic Eurorack module.

SamplerIns 03

Pitched or slicing. Old-skool repitching, per-step reverse, mic recording.

Drum SynthIns 04

x0x-style percussion.

04

Signal path

11 inserts · 4 sends · 5 macros

Any instrument takes any number of insert effects, in any order — overdrive, filter, gater and eight more. Four sends run in parallel; up to five macros sit over the whole mix. Three layers of modulation point at any of it: an LFO per instrument, a drawn pattern lane, a song lane.

A groovebox you don't outgrow

Fun in the first five minutes. Still deep in the fifth year.
